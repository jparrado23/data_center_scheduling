01 · Purpose

The question behind the project

Can flexible AI workloads be moved to better execution times without violating the operational constraints of a data center—and can quantum-compatible methods produce useful schedules for that problem?

Imagine an operator who knows the training jobs that must finish tomorrow. Each job requests a particular amount of computing capacity, runs for a fixed duration, and has a window within which it can start. Electricity prices and solar availability change through the day. Starting every job immediately may be costly. Moving all flexible work into the cheapest hours may overload the available GPUs. A useful scheduler must resolve both pressures together.

The first objective of this thesis is operational: build an energy-aware scheduling model that makes those trade-offs explicit. The second objective is methodological: extract a reduced binary problem, solve it through different computational approaches, and measure what is preserved or lost in that translation.

This gives the research two connected questions. How well can the classical model schedule the workload? And can a smaller quantum representation retain enough of the scheduling structure to produce feasible, economically useful decisions? The repository already supports experiments on both questions, although they are at different levels of maturity.

The current contribution is a classical reference, an auditable reduced formulation, and experiments that expose the practical limits of feasibility and scale. The saved evidence does not establish quantum advantage.

02 · Problem statement

From an operational story to a scheduling problem

The scheduler chooses a start slot and a compatible compute partition for every flexible job. A partition is an aggregate pool of computing resources. This abstraction lets the model reason about GPU, CPU, memory, and power limits without representing every physical server.

A job is non-preemptive: once it starts, its resource demand remains active throughout its specified duration. Its allowed start window limits how far it can move. Compatibility rules remove options that require the wrong GPU type or otherwise do not fit the partition. Several individually valid choices can still conflict when they overlap.

Inputs and outputs

Known before solvingChosen or derived by the model
Job duration, demand, power, and start windowsOne start time and partition for every job
Partition resources and compatibilityResource occupancy in every slot
Electricity prices, renewable supply, and PUEFacility load, renewable use, and grid import
Peak tariff and optional battery settingsPeak excess and optional storage dispatch

The distinction between IT power and facility power matters. GPUs and other computing equipment create IT load. Cooling and other overhead increase the electricity required by the facility. The model expresses that through Power Usage Effectiveness, or PUE.

Facility load(t) = PUE(t) × IT load(t)

The current experiments are static: jobs and energy inputs are supplied for a fixed horizon. They do not establish a live scheduler that reacts to new arrivals or forecast errors. Aggregate feasibility also does not guarantee that a low-level scheduler can pack every task onto individual machines. That is a separate placement problem.

03 · Classical foundation

Why the classical model comes first

The mixed-integer linear program, or MILP, gives the project a precise reference. Its binary decisions express discrete job placements, while continuous variables represent energy flows and optional battery operation. Gurobi searches for good feasible schedules and bounds how far the best known solution may be from the optimum.

x[i,k,s] = 1 if job i starts on partition k at slot s
Sum of x[i,k,s] over allowed (k,s) = 1 for every job i

The second line is the exactly-one assignment rule. Capacity constraints then add the demands of all jobs active in a slot and compare the total with the available resources. Start-window and compatibility restrictions are encoded in the set of allowed decisions.

Energy dispatch connects the schedule to its physical cost. Without storage, renewable consumption and grid import must cover facility demand. Renewable use is limited by availability. Its economic treatment matters: if the assumed renewable price exceeds the grid price, consuming every available unit of renewable energy is not necessarily optimal under the chosen cost objective.

Minimize renewable cost + grid cost + excess grid-import peak cost

The contracted-power threshold is a billing threshold on grid import. It is not a hard cap on total facility power. The optional battery adds charging, discharging, efficiency, and state-of-charge equations. The documented battery model is simplified: it omits degradation cost and does not explicitly prohibit simultaneous charging and discharging through a binary exclusion rule.

What a solver result actually tells us

A feasible incumbent is a schedule that satisfies the model. A bound limits the best objective that any solution could achieve. The MIP gap measures the remaining difference between the incumbent and that bound under the solver's definition. Reaching a time limit with a small gap means that a good schedule exists but its optimality has not been certified to a tighter tolerance.

This distinction is central to the thesis. Difficulty in proving optimality is different from difficulty in finding a usable schedule. Both should be recorded rather than reduced to a single runtime number.

04 · Data and validation

Build realism and difficulty deliberately

The repository combines workload-trace exploration with controlled synthetic experiments. Alibaba, Kalos, and Seren analyses provide information about workload and resource patterns. They do not supply every quantity required by an energy model. Electrical power, PUE, tariffs, renewable generation, and scheduling flexibility require additional inputs or explicit assumptions.

This is why trace-derived and synthetic instances play complementary roles. Trace-derived workloads connect the study to observed computing activity. Synthetic workloads let the researcher vary overlap, job size, duration, and scheduling windows while retaining control over feasibility and experimental difficulty.

Feasible-by-construction generators produce a hidden schedule as a certificate that an instance can be scheduled. The certificate is a validation aid; the solver should not receive it as an unfair shortcut in the intended comparison.

The research progression

  1. Hand-checkable cases: inspect assignment, capacity, energy balance, and objective behavior on very small examples.
  2. Trace exploration: identify workload characteristics and clarify which energy quantities remain assumed.
  3. Energy scenarios: compare clear-sky, overcast, and base profiles using consistent physical inputs within each experiment.
  4. Stress generation: introduce longer jobs, multi-node demands, overlapping windows, and competition for attractive energy periods.
  5. Benchmark ladder: track graph size and solver behavior as scheduling structure becomes more challenging.

Changing from hourly to 15-minute slots improves temporal resolution but can create many more candidate starts. Extending job durations also couples decisions across more slots. A small number of jobs can therefore create a large and densely interacting binary problem.

05 · The reduction

What is simplified for quantum-compatible optimization?

The full operational model contains discrete schedules and continuous energy decisions. Encoding every physical feature into binary variables would introduce additional variables, precision choices, and interactions. The project therefore extracts the discrete scheduling core first.

The reduced model retains binary job-start decisions. It keeps exactly-one assignment and aggregate GPU capacity through penalties. Compatibility is handled structurally by excluding disallowed choices. An effective hourly price makes some start times economically more attractive, and an optional squared-load term discourages concentrated demand.

FeatureFull MILPReduced QUBO
Job starts and compatibilityExplicit allowed binary choicesSame kind of allowed binary choices
Exactly one startHard equality constraintSquared penalty
GPU capacityHard capacity inequalitySquared equality with binary slack
CPU and memoryAggregate constraintsNo equivalent overlapping-load penalties
Renewable/grid dispatchExplicit physical energy flowsEffective-price approximation
Grid-import peak billingMaximum import and excess chargeOptional load-smoothing proxy
Battery operationOptional explicit modelOmitted

An effective price blends the economic attraction of renewable and grid energy using an estimate of the renewable share at a representative load scale. It gives each job-start option a fixed energy coefficient. Actual renewable allocation, however, depends on how all jobs overlap. A fixed coefficient cannot reproduce that interaction exactly.

Consequently, the schedule must be decoded and evaluated again using physical renewable availability and grid prices. Common cost evaluation helps compare outcomes, but does not make the two optimization objectives identical. Nor does an assignment-and-GPU audit by itself establish all the constraints of the full MILP.

The reduction is part of the experiment. Differences in physical cost can arise from the solver, the reduced feasible set, or the approximate objective.

06 · QUBO mechanics

How scheduling becomes an energy function

A quadratic unconstrained binary optimization problem, or QUBO, assigns an energy to every binary vector. The solver searches for vectors with low energy. Constraints are represented by positive penalties, so the word unconstrained describes the solver interface rather than the original scheduling problem.

H = H_energy + λ_assignment H_assignment
    + λ_GPU H_GPU + λ_peak H_peak

The energy term rewards economically attractive start options. The assignment term penalizes scheduling a job zero times or more than once. For a job with candidate decisions x₁, x₂, and x₃, that term is:

H_assignment,job = (x₁ + x₂ + x₃ − 1)²

It vanishes when exactly one choice is selected. Expanding the square creates linear terms and pairwise couplings. Because a binary variable satisfies x² = x, the expression remains quadratic.

GPU capacity is an inequality. Binary slack represents unused capacity, allowing the builder to penalize an equality instead:

GPU load(k,t) + unused capacity(k,t) = GPU capacity(k)
H_GPU = sum over (k,t) of the squared equality residual

Slack bits are auxiliary representation variables, not extra jobs. They can consume a substantial fraction of the QUBO. A decoded schedule may fit physical GPU capacity even when its sampled slack bits are inconsistent. The project therefore distinguishes schedule feasibility from full QUBO penalty satisfaction.

Penalty selection is consequential. Weak penalties may allow invalid schedules to appear attractive. Very large penalties can overwhelm the energy differences that distinguish useful schedules and change the numerical landscape seen by the solver. Calibration should be performed on development instances, followed by evaluation on held-out targets.

The peak term, when enabled, penalizes squared facility load across slots. It encourages spreading work but does not directly implement the maximum grid-import charge. Some current comparisons disable it entirely; those results should not be interpreted as successful peak optimization.

Annealing

Simulated annealing changes candidate bits while controlling the willingness to accept energy-increasing moves. Multiple reads provide multiple candidate solutions. D-Wave software branding does not make this execution quantum: the saved local baseline is classical simulated annealing. Physical annealing and hybrid services are separate execution paths whose use needs separate evidence.

Tensor networks

The implementation expresses the QUBO as local energy factors and eliminates binary variables by minimizing over them. It is an exact zero-temperature, min-sum computation when completed. Intermediate factors can become large as elimination connects neighboring variables. The important scale is therefore not just the initial bit count, but also the width of the interaction structure during elimination.

A fair comparison

Report feasibility before cost, distinguish raw from repaired outputs, and include the computational budget. A useful future comparison should also measure how well a simple classical scheduling heuristic or the same repair strategy performs without quantum correlations. That would help isolate the value contributed by the PCE representation itself.

08 · Quantum compression

PCE: fewer qubits, a harder decoding question

A direct QAOA representation normally assigns one logical qubit to each binary QUBO variable. Hundreds of job-start and slack decisions quickly create a demanding circuit width. Pauli Correlation Encoding, or PCE, explores a different representation: associate binary decisions with signs of measured Pauli correlations on a smaller register.

First, the QUBO is converted into an equivalent Ising polynomial using zᵢ = (1 + sᵢ)/2. Each spin is then associated with a Pauli observable. In the project's order-two construction, the available observables include XX, YY, and ZZ on each pair of qubits.

Available observables = 3 × n(n − 1) / 2
Choose the smallest n whose observable count covers all QUBO variables

The saved tiny example contains 25 assignment variables and 40 slack variables: 65 binary variables in total. Eight PCE qubits provide 84 such observable slots. This demonstrates representation capacity. It does not show that every desirable assignment of signs is independently reachable by the selected circuit, or that the optimizer can find it.

From correlations to a schedule

A parameterized circuit prepares a state. Its Pauli expectations become continuous proxies for the binary spins. During training, a smooth transformation such as tanh(αrᵢ) makes the objective optimizable. At decoding, signs produce discrete bits. A good relaxed objective can therefore coexist with an invalid decoded schedule.

Progressive-binarization PCE increases the binarization parameter through successive optimization stages. It aims to bridge continuous optimization and discrete decisions. The project also examines fixed-alpha runs, different circuit constructions, and the history of candidate evaluations rather than only the optimizer's final point.

Classical repair has a specific, limited role

The current duplicate-only repair requires every job to be present and the raw candidate to respect physical GPU capacity. It keeps the lowest-effective-cost selected assignment for each duplicated job, then validates the resulting schedule again. It does not invent an assignment for a missing job. The procedure restores raw GPU units before physical validation, which matters when node or block units were used during optimization.

Any successful repaired schedule should be labeled as a hybrid result. Counting it as a directly feasible PCE output would hide the contribution of classical post-processing.

The displayed sixteen configurations each use 120 theta evaluations and record zero directly feasible candidates and zero duplicate-only physical candidates. 4. The orchestrator shows why all metrics are needed

For the saved synthetic-duration complex case in notebook 24, Gurobi returns feasible solutions on all three energy days in approximately 0.05–0.64 seconds. Local annealing takes approximately 28.5–29.0 seconds, finding no feasible sample on the base or clear-sky day and one feasible sample out of 1,000 on the overcast day.

On that overcast case, the physically evaluated cost is 921.38 for the annealing schedule versus 843.84 for the Gurobi schedule: approximately 9.19% higher. This is a comparison with the Gurobi incumbent, whose reported gap is about 0.0923%, rather than a certified exact-optimum difference. PCE is skipped for this 1,436-variable QUBO under notebook 24's configured statevector limit.

The notebook's peak-smoothing coefficient is zero, and its contracted-power threshold normally makes excess-peak billing inactive. These rows therefore isolate energy scheduling and feasibility; they do not establish peak-charge reduction.

10 · Interpretation

What we can claim—and what still needs evidence

The repository supports a substantive progress claim: the project has moved from problem definition to a reusable classical model, a reduced binary encoding, controlled workloads, and experiments that diagnose solver behavior. Its strongest current evidence concerns formulation, representation cost, and feasibility.

Several stronger claims would require further work. Qubit reduction is not equivalent to runtime reduction. A simulator experiment is not a hardware benchmark. A physically feasible repaired schedule is not a directly feasible quantum output. A low QUBO energy is not automatically a low electricity bill.

Separate three sources of error

  1. Model reduction: the effective-price objective and omitted physical features change what is optimized.
  2. Solver search: the selected method may not find a good low-energy binary candidate within its budget.
  3. Representation and decoding: continuous correlations may not produce the desired discrete assignments, even after the relaxed loss improves.

Useful experiments isolate those effects. Comparing methods on one frozen QUBO separates solver behavior from coefficient changes. Evaluating every valid schedule with a common physical cost function exposes surrogate error. Comparing direct and repaired candidates exposes the role of post-processing.

Notebook-specific limits must also remain explicit. Notebook 24 uses a conservative 16-qubit statevector cap, whereas notebook 23 contains saved larger simulations. The cap is a chosen execution budget, not a universal physical limit. Likewise, no QPU submission in notebook 24 is evidence about that notebook's saved run, not an exhaustive audit of every external account.

Some older documentation understates current implementation progress, and some notebook prose differs from displayed circuit selections or later output. This document gives concrete saved tables and source code priority while treating the collection as a research snapshot rather than one fully synchronized benchmark campaign.

11 · Next steps

A concrete path to the next thesis milestone

Consolidate the experiment record

Export a common results table with instance identifier, input version, resource units, energy day, all penalty weights, seed, circuit architecture, optimizer budget, sampling budget, runtime, feasibility status, repair status, and physical cost. Keep failed and skipped rows. Record Gurobi bounds alongside incumbents. This would turn the existing notebook evidence into an easier-to-audit thesis experiment set.

Make feasibility the next controlled study

Use the diagnostics to distinguish missing jobs, duplicated assignments, and capacity excess. Compare fixed-alpha and progressive-binarization settings on a fixed development set with repeated initializations. Record candidate coverage across the whole optimization trace. Evaluate selected settings on held-out instances rather than tuning against the final hard target.

Measure how much repair contributes

Report raw feasibility and repaired feasibility separately, including repair time and the number of removed assignments. Introduce practical classical scheduling baselines and compare the same post-processing on classical candidates. This is necessary to establish whether PCE adds value beyond the repair mechanism.

Study objective fidelity and graph growth

Measure how ranking schedules by effective price differs from ranking them by physical energy cost. Run peak-sensitive comparisons only when the peak objective is deliberately active. Track assignment variables, slack bits, couplers, and intermediate tensor factors as the time resolution and window width change.

Validate hardware only after the selected simulator experiment is useful

The optional IBM path evaluates a statevector-trained circuit using hardware correlations. Its initial question is whether measurement noise changes decoded feasibility and schedule quality. Hardware-in-the-loop training is a further experiment with repeated quantum evaluations, shot cost, and queue or execution overhead.

Return to the operational setting

A future rolling-horizon scheduler would update arrivals and forecasts, preserve running or committed jobs, and optimize a moving window. Hybrid decomposition could extract selected difficult subproblems for QUBO methods while classical optimization manages the wider system. This is a deployment direction, not an implemented capability established by the current static benchmarks.

The next milestone is a reproducible account of when compressed methods produce valid schedules, what those schedules cost, and how much classical assistance is required.
