01 · Purpose

Testing quantum methods on constrained AI scheduling

The goal of this thesis is to test whether quantum optimization methods can produce feasible, useful 24-hour schedules for flexible AI workloads, and to understand how their performance changes as scheduling constraints and problem size become more demanding.

The scientific question is not whether Gurobi can generate a schedule. Classical mathematical optimization already provides a strong way to do that. Gurobi is the reference against which we measure feasibility, physical energy cost, and solution quality. Beating it is not a prerequisite for a useful thesis, and the present results do not suggest that it has been beaten.

The focus is the behavior of quantum-compatible scheduling formulations: which constraints survive encoding and decoding, how many variables and qubits are required, and whether valid schedules remain obtainable as we add jobs, candidate starts, clusters, and resource conflicts. PCE is the gate-based quantum method under investigation. Local simulated annealing supplies a classical baseline on the same QUBO.

The research questions

  1. Feasibility: can the quantum method schedule every job once, within its allowed window, without exceeding capacity? How often is classical repair needed?
  2. Practical value: do its feasible schedules reduce physical energy cost relative to a simple FIFO policy, and how close are they to the Gurobi reference?
  3. Scaling: what happens to feasibility, quality, runtime, circuit width, and measurement burden as the problem structure becomes more complex?

FIFO is a useful proposed comparison because it represents a simple dispatch rule that does not optimize energy. Improving on FIFO would demonstrate value over that particular policy; it would not establish quantum advantage over strong classical optimization. FIFO has not yet been implemented or benchmarked in the active experiment workflow.

The hypothesis is that quantum methods may remain useful on larger constrained schedules as algorithms and hardware improve. More available qubits alone do not establish that hypothesis: trainability, noise, connectivity, measurement cost, and decoded feasibility also have to be tested.

02 · Problem statement

The problem: an energy-aware schedule for the next 24 hours

Given a set of flexible AI jobs, compute-cluster capacities, electricity prices, and solar availability, choose when and where every job runs during the next 24 hours so that all scheduling constraints are respected and electricity cost is minimized.

The output is a complete day-ahead schedule, not just a recommended start time for one job. Each job must start within its allowed window, stay on one compatible cluster, run without interruption, and finish within the horizon. At every slot, the total demand of overlapping jobs must fit the cluster's modeled resource limits.

Energy consumption and energy cost are different objectives

The broad operational aim is better energy use. The objective currently implemented is energy cost, optionally including a grid-import peak charge. With fixed job powers and durations, executing the same jobs consumes the same flexible IT energy regardless of their start times. With fixed PUE and fixed baseline load, total facility energy is also fixed. Moving jobs toward solar availability can reduce grid imports and cost without reducing total facility MWh.

E_IT,flex = Δt × Σᵢ pᵢdᵢ
E_facility = Δt × Σₜ ηₜ(Bₜ + scheduled IT loadₜ)

If PUE varies with time as an externally supplied profile, moving a job to a lower-PUE period can also reduce facility energy. Minimizing facility MWh would nevertheless be a different objective from minimizing cost. In this document, cost is the primary objective consistent with the implementation; facility energy, grid energy, solar use, and peak import are reported separately.

What must be supplied?

InputMeaning
JobsDuration, IT power, resource demand, compatible clusters, and earliest/latest start
ClustersGPU capacity and, when enabled, aggregate CPU, memory, and IT power limits
Energy daySolar availability, grid price, renewable price assumption, and fixed or time-varying PUE
Time resolution24 one-hour slots or 96 fifteen-minute slots, covering the same 24 hours

For example, a job may be allowed to run in either a morning or afternoon window. Solar availability might favor midday, but other jobs may already need that capacity. The scheduler must find the best feasible combination across all jobs, rather than selecting the cheapest hour independently for each one.

Battery operation is outside the revised thesis scope. The model is deterministic and static: inputs are known for the horizon. Cluster capacities are aggregate planning constraints; the model does not assign individual GPUs to physical machines or react to new arrivals during execution.

03 · Classical foundation

The battery-free mathematical formulation

Gurobi solves the reference formulation below. Its role is to provide a strong benchmark and, where available, an optimality bound. The formulation includes binary scheduling decisions and continuous electricity-supply variables. All power quantities use MW, energy uses MWh, and Δt is measured in hours.

1. Sets and allowed decisions

I = set of jobs; K = set of compute clusters
T = {0, …, N−1}; NΔt = 24
Δt ∈ {1, 0.25}; N ∈ {24, 96}
Kᵢ = clusters compatible with job i
Sᵢ = {s ∈ T : eᵢ ≤ s ≤ ℓᵢ and s + dᵢ ≤ N}
Ω = {(i,k,s) : i ∈ I, k ∈ Kᵢ, s ∈ Sᵢ}
Aᵢₛₜ = 1 if s ≤ t < s+dᵢ, and 0 otherwise

Here eᵢ and ℓᵢ are earliest and latest start slots, and dᵢ is the duration in slots. If a completion deadline Dᵢ is supplied instead of a latest start, use ℓᵢ = Dᵢ − dᵢ. Compatibility incorporates the permitted cluster relation and relevant individual resource requirements. Non-preemption and remaining on one cluster are enforced by selecting one start whose activity spans all dᵢ slots.

2. Parameters

SymbolDefinition
pᵢ, gᵢ, cᵢ, mᵢJob IT power, GPU demand, CPU demand, and memory demand
Pₖ, Gₖ, Cₖ, MₖCluster IT-power, GPU, CPU, and memory capacities
BₜFixed site IT baseline load; zero when unused
S̄ₜAvailable solar power
πᵍₜ, πʳGrid and renewable prices per MWh
ηₜPUE, supplied as a fixed scalar or external time profile
Pᶜ, πᵖContracted grid-import threshold and excess-peak charge per MW

GPU demands and capacities must use matching units. If eight-GPU nodes are used, convert both consistently. CPU and memory constraints apply when those resources are enabled and populated; a GPU-focused experiment must explicitly document which other limits are inactive or nonbinding.

3. Decision variables

xᵢₖₛ ∈ {0,1}, for (i,k,s) ∈ Ω
Rₜ ≥ 0 : solar/renewable power consumed
Qₜ ≥ 0 : grid power imported
Pᵖᵉᵃᵏ ≥ 0 : upper bound on grid-import peak
Eᵖᵉᵃᵏ ≥ 0 : import above the contracted threshold

The peak variables can be omitted when peak billing is excluded. With a positive peak coefficient, minimization makes Eᵖᵉᵃᵏ equal to the positive excess of maximum grid import over the contracted threshold.

4. Exactly one assignment

Σₖ∈Kᵢ Σₛ∈Sᵢ xᵢₖₛ = 1                         ∀ i ∈ I

No variable is created outside Ω. This excludes incompatible clusters, disallowed starts, and jobs extending beyond the horizon.

5. Resource limits in every cluster and slot

Σᵢ Σₛ:(i,k,s)∈Ω gᵢ Aᵢₛₜ xᵢₖₛ ≤ Gₖ          ∀ k,t
Σᵢ Σₛ:(i,k,s)∈Ω pᵢ Aᵢₛₜ xᵢₖₛ ≤ Pₖ          ∀ k,t
Σᵢ Σₛ:(i,k,s)∈Ω cᵢ Aᵢₛₜ xᵢₖₛ ≤ Cₖ          ∀ k,t [if enabled]
Σᵢ Σₛ:(i,k,s)∈Ω mᵢ Aᵢₛₜ xᵢₖₛ ≤ Mₖ          ∀ k,t [if enabled]

The fixed baseline Bₜ contributes to site energy balance. In the current implementation it is not allocated to cluster resource constraints; reserved cluster resources must therefore be reflected separately in available capacities if such a baseline is introduced.

6. Fixed and time-varying PUE

L_IT,ₜ(x) = Bₜ + Σ₍ᵢ,ₖ,ₛ₎∈Ω pᵢ Aᵢₛₜ xᵢₖₛ
L_facility,ₜ(x) = ηₜ L_IT,ₜ(x)
Fixed PUE: ηₜ = η for all t
Time-varying PUE: ηₜ is a known coefficient for each t

Both cases are linear because PUE is input data. “Dynamic PUE” here means an external time-varying profile, not a PUE function of decision-dependent load. A load-dependent PUE would require a separate formulation and is not assumed here.

7. Energy-source balance and solar availability

Rₜ + Qₜ = L_facility,ₜ(x)                      ∀ t
0 ≤ Rₜ ≤ S̄ₜ; Qₜ ≥ 0                          ∀ t

There is no battery, inter-slot storage equation, or export variable. Unused solar is curtailed. Solar utilization is chosen economically under its stated price rather than automatically forced.

8. Optional peak billing

Pᵖᵉᵃᵏ ≥ Qₜ                                    ∀ t
Eᵖᵉᵃᵏ ≥ Pᵖᵉᵃᵏ − Pᶜ; Eᵖᵉᵃᵏ ≥ 0

This is a tariff threshold on grid import, not a hard physical facility cap. In the saved notebook 24 comparisons, the threshold normally makes the excess charge inactive. If peak billing is excluded from the experiment, set πᵖ = 0 and omit these auxiliary variables.

9. Objective

minₓ,ᴿ,ᑫ,ᴾ,ᴱ  Δt Σₜ (πʳ Rₜ + πᵍₜ Qₜ) + πᵖ Eᵖᵉᵃᵏ

For a consumption-only study, the alternative objective would be min Δt Σₜ L_facility,ₜ(x). With constant PUE, fixed job powers/durations, and all jobs completed, this quantity is constant, so it cannot distinguish schedules. The cost objective is therefore retained for the present comparison.

10. Why this is still a MILP without batteries

The scheduling variables are binary, but renewable and grid quantities are continuous. Removing storage does not change that fact. One can eliminate energy dispatch analytically under suitable assumptions and optimize only over binary schedules, but the resulting physical-cost function is generally piecewise linear in load rather than a simple linear binary objective. The next chapter explains how the QUBO uses fixed price coefficients to simplify that dependence.

04 · Data and validation

Three input families and why stress instances are necessary

The experiment design uses three input families: solar/price profiles, Seren-derived workloads, and controlled synthetic workloads. They answer different questions and should be reported separately.

1. Solar availability and electricity prices

Use the three representative energy days—clear sky, base, and overcast—to evaluate scheduling under different amounts and timing of solar supply. Each case also includes its grid-price profile, a stated renewable-price assumption, and PUE. Hold these inputs identical across solvers within the same workload/day comparison.

Because grid prices also differ between the saved representative days, a difference in cost across days is not attributable to solar alone. If the aim is to isolate solar sensitivity, add a controlled comparison that holds prices and workload fixed while changing only solar availability. This is a proposed ablation, not a result already established.

2. Seren traces: the empirical workload basis

Seren is the workload trace selected for the thesis. Alibaba and Kalos explorations remain historical repository material but are not part of the active comparison described here. Seren informs the workload characteristics used to construct scheduling instances; electrical power, energy profiles, and allowed scheduling flexibility still require explicit preparation or assumptions.

Time resolution matters. One-hour slots can sharply reduce candidate start choices and hide short-timescale conflicts. Fifteen-minute slots provide 96 decision periods over the same day, allow more starts, and preserve more timing detail. They are therefore included in the study of richer scheduling structure. Finer resolution is not by itself a guarantee of solver difficulty.

Durations, start windows, and energy inputs must be converted consistently. Rounding durations differently between resolutions changes the modeled workload; interpolating hourly solar into quarter-hour values does not create observed high-frequency solar data. Those choices should be recorded when comparing resolutions.

3. Synthetic traces: controlled capacity stress

In the selected low-pressure Seren cases, the configured cluster has much more GPU capacity than the jobs require. When GPU capacity is the principal resource constraint, many job combinations can fit simultaneously. Such instances are useful for verifying the pipeline and energy response, but they provide limited evidence about solving difficult resource conflicts.

Synthetic instances deliberately increase multi-node demands, overlapping windows, job durations, and competition for attractive energy periods relative to the fixed capacity. This creates scheduling decisions that cannot all be satisfied by choosing the individually cheapest start. Existing feasible-by-construction generators retain a hidden witness schedule to certify that the instance is schedulable.

The relevance is methodological: a constrained solver should be tested where its constraints actually bind. Synthetic cases let us vary one source of difficulty at a time and distinguish a solver limitation from an easy workload. They are controlled stress tests, not evidence that the same congestion is observed in Seren or that every real data center operates at that pressure.

How to report the experiment matrix

AxisPurpose
Seren versus synthetic workloadEmpirical workload basis versus controlled resource conflict
Clear sky / base / overcastResponse to representative energy-day conditions
1 h versus 15 minTiming detail and candidate-start growth
Demand, overlap, duration, compatibilityControlled variation in combinatorial structure

Record utilization and observed capacity conflicts rather than labeling all Seren cases easy. Likewise, adding clusters may increase choices while relieving congestion; it does not necessarily make an instance harder. Difficulty must be measured through actual solver behavior.

05 · The reduction

From the physical model to the scheduling QUBO

The battery-free model still contains two kinds of decisions: binary job starts and continuous amounts of solar and grid electricity. The QUBO keeps job starts and replaces explicit energy dispatch with a fixed estimated price for each slot. It also replaces hard scheduling constraints with penalties.

Why the physical cost depends on the whole schedule

Consider one slot with 1 MWh of available solar, solar costing 20 per MWh, grid electricity costing 100 per MWh, and no peak charge. Suppose two jobs each need 1 MWh of facility energy in that slot. Either job alone costs 20. Both together cost 120 because the solar supply can cover only one of the two MWh. Assigning a fixed coefficient of 20 to each job would incorrectly predict a joint cost of 40.

This illustrative example explains the approximation: the cost of a start depends on what else is scheduled alongside it. A fixed effective price estimates that effect without explicitly allocating renewable and grid supply for every candidate schedule.

Without active peak billing, if πʳ ≤ πᵍₜ:
Rₜ(x) = min(S̄ₜ, L_facility,ₜ(x))
Qₜ(x) = max(0, L_facility,ₜ(x) − S̄ₜ)
Costₜ(x) = Δt [πʳ Rₜ(x) + πᵍₜ Qₜ(x)]

These expressions eliminate dispatch variables but leave a piecewise cost function of binary choices. If renewable power is more expensive and there is no active peak charge, grid-only dispatch is cheaper. With active peak billing, dispatch and the maximum grid import must be optimized consistently. Thus binary scheduling decisions do not automatically imply a binary linear formulation with the same physical objective.

How we interpret differences in results

If a QUBO schedule costs more than Gurobi's, there are two distinct possibilities: the solver found a poor solution to the QUBO, or it found a good solution to an approximate objective that ranks schedules differently from the physical model. If it violates an omitted resource constraint, it also fails the broader reference feasibility requirements.

We therefore decode the schedule, check the relevant constraints, and calculate physical energy cost from actual solar availability and grid prices. For a GPU-focused comparison, other constraints must either be checked or shown to be nonbinding. A low QUBO energy alone is never the final performance metric.

06 · QUBO mechanics

How scheduling becomes an energy function

A quadratic unconstrained binary optimization problem, or QUBO, assigns an energy to every binary vector. The solver searches for vectors with low energy. Constraints are represented by positive penalties, so the word unconstrained describes the solver interface rather than the original scheduling problem.

H = H_energy + λ_assignment H_assignment
    + λ_GPU H_GPU + λ_peak H_peak

The energy term rewards economically attractive start options. The assignment term penalizes scheduling a job zero times or more than once. For a job with candidate decisions x₁, x₂, and x₃, that term is:

H_assignment,job = (x₁ + x₂ + x₃ − 1)²

It vanishes when exactly one choice is selected. Expanding the square creates linear terms and pairwise couplings. Because a binary variable satisfies x² = x, the expression remains quadratic.

GPU capacity is an inequality. Binary slack represents unused capacity, allowing the builder to penalize an equality instead:

GPU load(k,t) + unused capacity(k,t) = GPU capacity(k)
H_GPU = sum over (k,t) of the squared equality residual

Slack bits are auxiliary representation variables, not extra jobs. They can consume a substantial fraction of the QUBO. A decoded schedule may fit physical GPU capacity even when its sampled slack bits are inconsistent. The project therefore distinguishes schedule feasibility from full QUBO penalty satisfaction.

Penalty selection is consequential. Weak penalties may allow invalid schedules to appear attractive. Very large penalties can overwhelm the energy differences that distinguish useful schedules and change the numerical landscape seen by the solver. Calibration should be performed on development instances, followed by evaluation on held-out targets.

The peak term, when enabled, penalizes squared facility load across slots. It encourages spreading work but does not directly implement the maximum grid-import charge. Some current comparisons disable it entirely; those results should not be interpreted as successful peak optimization.

The active comparison: Gurobi, annealing, and PCE

The active notebook workflow compares three methods. Gurobi provides the classical reference, simulated annealing tests the QUBO with a classical search method, and PCE tests the compressed quantum representation. Tensor networks are outside the selected thesis comparison, although their historical source module remains in the repository.

MethodQuestion it answersCurrent status
Gurobi / MILPWhat feasible physical cost and optimality bound can a strong classical optimizer achieve?Implemented; saved comparison results
Simulated annealingCan classical sampling of this QUBO produce feasible, useful schedules?Implemented using the classical D-Wave Ocean sampler
PCE, including PB-PCECan a compressed correlation encoding preserve useful constrained scheduling decisions?Implemented statevector experiments, diagnostics, and limited repair

Gurobi: reference quality and bounds

A feasible incumbent is a complete schedule satisfying the reference constraints. The solver bound and MIP gap describe the remaining uncertainty about optimality. Gurobi's ability to schedule is not the novelty; it gives the comparison a credible reference.

Annealing: the same binary encoding without a quantum circuit

Local simulated annealing searches candidate bitstrings while changing its willingness to accept energy-increasing moves. Multiple reads yield multiple samples. This is classical computation, despite the D-Wave software name. It helps reveal whether feasibility issues occur already in the QUBO search or become more severe under PCE representation and decoding.

PCE: the quantum-method experiment

PCE represents binary variables through Pauli expectations on a smaller circuit. PB-PCE varies the binarization strength over successive optimization stages. The selected statevector simulations test this algorithm class on classical hardware; optional IBM validation is a separate execution stage, not a different scheduling method.

A reproducible FIFO baseline

A suitable proposed policy is strict FCFS/FIFO: sort jobs by release time, break ties deterministically by job ID, and dispatch the head-of-line job at the first slot where a compatible cluster has enough capacity for its entire non-preemptive duration. Do not let later jobs overtake it. Use a fixed cluster-ID tie-break when multiple clusters fit. If release timestamps are unavailable, declare earliest allowed start as the ordering proxy.

If the head-of-line job cannot fit within its window, flag the run as unable to produce a complete feasible schedule. Do not silently drop that job or compare a partial schedule's cost with a complete optimized schedule. Backfilling could be tested separately, but should be named as a different heuristic.

Evaluate FIFO with the same jobs, energy profiles, PUE, capacities, and physical cost evaluator. Report complete-schedule feasibility first, then cost, grid energy, peak import, and runtime. Compute cost improvement as 100 × (C_FIFO − C_method) / C_FIFO only when both schedules are feasible and the denominator is positive.

Improvement over FIFO would support practical value over a simple energy-unaware policy. It would not by itself show that the quantum component is responsible: annealing, Gurobi, and a future energy-aware classical heuristic provide complementary comparisons.

08 · Quantum compression

PCE: fewer qubits, a harder decoding question

A direct QAOA representation normally assigns one logical qubit to each binary QUBO variable. Hundreds of job-start and slack decisions quickly create a demanding circuit width. Pauli Correlation Encoding, or PCE, explores a different representation: associate binary decisions with signs of measured Pauli correlations on a smaller register.

First, the QUBO is converted into an equivalent Ising polynomial using zᵢ = (1 + sᵢ)/2. Each spin is then associated with a Pauli observable. In the project's order-two construction, the available observables include XX, YY, and ZZ on each pair of qubits.

Available observables = 3 × n(n − 1) / 2
Choose the smallest n whose observable count covers all QUBO variables

The saved tiny example contains 25 assignment variables and 40 slack variables: 65 binary variables in total. Eight PCE qubits provide 84 such observable slots. This demonstrates representation capacity. It does not show that every desirable assignment of signs is independently reachable by the selected circuit, or that the optimizer can find it.

From correlations to a schedule

A parameterized circuit prepares a state. Its Pauli expectations become continuous proxies for the binary spins. During training, a smooth transformation such as tanh(αrᵢ) makes the objective optimizable. At decoding, signs produce discrete bits. A good relaxed objective can therefore coexist with an invalid decoded schedule.

Progressive-binarization PCE increases the binarization parameter through successive optimization stages. It aims to bridge continuous optimization and discrete decisions. The project also examines fixed-alpha runs, different circuit constructions, and the history of candidate evaluations rather than only the optimizer's final point.

Classical repair has a specific, limited role

The current duplicate-only repair requires every job to be present and the raw candidate to respect physical GPU capacity. It keeps the lowest-effective-cost selected assignment for each duplicated job, then validates the resulting schedule again. It does not invent an assignment for a missing job. The procedure restores raw GPU units before physical validation, which matters when node or block units were used during optimization.

Any successful repaired schedule should be labeled as a hybrid result. Counting it as a directly feasible PCE output would hide the contribution of classical post-processing.

Notes and feedback · 09 · Evidence so far
10 · Interpretation

What we can claim—and what still needs evidence

The quantum-method study supports a substantive progress claim: the project has moved from problem definition to a classical reference, a reduced binary encoding, controlled workloads, and experiments that diagnose quantum-method feasibility and scale. Its strongest current evidence concerns representation cost and feasibility. Establishing that Gurobi can schedule is supporting infrastructure, not the research contribution.

Several stronger claims would require further work. Qubit reduction is not equivalent to runtime reduction. A simulator experiment is not a hardware benchmark. A physically feasible repaired schedule is not a directly feasible quantum output. A low QUBO energy is not automatically a low electricity bill.

Separate three sources of error

  1. Model reduction: the effective-price objective and omitted physical features change what is optimized.
  2. Solver search: the selected method may not find a good low-energy binary candidate within its budget.
  3. Representation and decoding: continuous correlations may not produce the desired discrete assignments, even after the relaxed loss improves.

Useful experiments isolate those effects. Comparing methods on one frozen QUBO separates solver behavior from coefficient changes. Evaluating every valid schedule with a common physical cost function exposes surrogate error. Comparing direct and repaired candidates exposes the role of post-processing.

Notebook-specific limits must also remain explicit. Notebook 24 uses a conservative 16-qubit statevector cap, whereas notebook 23 contains saved larger simulations. The cap is a chosen execution budget, not a universal physical limit. Likewise, no QPU submission in notebook 24 is evidence about that notebook's saved run, not an exhaustive audit of every external account.

Some older documentation understates current implementation progress, and some notebook prose differs from displayed circuit selections or later output. This document gives concrete saved tables and source code priority while treating the collection as a research snapshot rather than one fully synchronized benchmark campaign.

Consolidate the experiment record

Report raw feasibility and repaired feasibility separately, including repair time and the number of removed assignments. This is necessary to establish whether PCE adds value beyond the repair mechanism.

Study objective fidelity and graph growth

Measure how ranking schedules by effective price differs from ranking them by physical energy cost. Run peak-sensitive comparisons only when the peak objective is deliberately active. Track assignment variables, slack bits, couplers, and PCE qubit count as the time resolution and window width change.

Validate hardware only after the selected simulator experiment is useful

The optional IBM path evaluates a statevector-trained circuit using hardware correlations. Its initial question is whether measurement noise changes decoded feasibility and schedule quality. Hardware-in-the-loop training is a further experiment with repeated quantum evaluations, shot cost, and queue or execution overhead.

Return to the operational setting

A future rolling-horizon scheduler would update arrivals and forecasts, preserve running or committed jobs, and optimize a moving window. Hybrid decomposition could extract selected difficult subproblems for QUBO methods while classical optimization manages the wider system. This is a deployment direction, not an implemented capability established by the current static benchmarks.

The next milestone for the quantum-method study is a reproducible account of when compressed methods produce valid schedules, what those schedules cost, and how much classical assistance is required.

tests/Automated behavioral checks; presence is not a claim of a fresh test run
MILP
Mixed-integer linear program: discrete choices and continuous quantities under linear constraints.
QUBO
Quadratic unconstrained binary optimization: an energy function of binary variables.
Coupler
A coefficient connecting two binary variables in the quadratic objective.
Slack
An auxiliary quantity representing unused capacity.
PCE
Pauli Correlation Encoding: representation of classical decisions through quantum-state correlations.
PB-PCE
Progressive-binarization PCE: a continuation approach that changes the binarization strength during training.
Statevector simulation
Classical calculation of quantum-state amplitudes; computational requirements grow exponentially with circuit width.
Physical feasibility
In the reported reduced-schedule checks, valid assignments and GPU capacity. Additional full-model constraints require their own validation.
Incumbent and gap
The best feasible solution found and the remaining distance to a solver bound.
